## Learning an Efficient Multi-Turn Dialogue Evaluator from Multiple LLM Judges

### Abstract

Evaluating the conversational abilities of large language models (LLMs) remains a challenging task. Current mainstream approaches primarily rely on the “LLM-as-a-judge" paradigm, where an LLM is prompted to serve as an evaluator to assess dialogue quality. However, such methods often suffer from various biases, which undermine the reliability and consistency of the evaluation results. To mitigate these biases, recent methods employ multiple LLMs as judges and aggregate their judgments to select the optimal assessment. Although effective, this multi-judge approach incurs significant computational overhead during inference. In this paper, we propose an efficient dialogue evaluator that captures the collective wisdom of multiple LLM judges by aggregating their preference knowledge into a single model. Our approach preserves the advantages of diverse multi-judge feedback while drastically reducing the evaluation cost, enabling fast, flexible, and fine-grained dialogue quality assessment. Extensive experiments on seven single rating and pairwise comparison dialogue evaluation benchmarks demonstrate that our method outperforms existing baselines across diverse scenarios, showcasing its efficiency and robustness.

Traditional automated evaluation metrics (e.g., BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002), ROUGE (Lin, 2004), and BERTScore (Zhang et al., 2019)), which rely on fixed lexical or semantic overlaps, often fail to effectively capture the flexibility of natural language as perceived by humans, as well as the rich semantics present in multi-turn dialogues (Feng et al., 2025). Moreover, these metrics require external references to evaluate knowledgegrounded responses, limiting their applicability in scenarios where such references are unavailable.

In recent years, the “LLM-as-a-judge” paradigm (Zheng et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024, 2025a) has gained increasing attention for assessing response quality through single rating or pairwise comparison (Fig. 1). While proprietary LLM judges show high correlation with human judgments (Zheng et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2023), they often suffer from opaque training data, limited controllability, and high inference cost (Kim et al., 2024). To improve transparency and

deployability, recent works have explored training open-source LLM evaluators, typically by learning from preference data provided by a single judge (Sahoo et al., 2025; Li et al., 2023a; Zhu et al.,

- 2023). However, these learned evaluators still lag behind in robustness and scalability, and singlejudge annotation is known to exhibit systematic biases such as self-preference, score compression, and prompt sensitivity (Thakur et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2024a). To mitigate these biases, recent methods (Rahmani et al., 2024; Verga et al., 2024; Sun

- et al., 2024a) employ multiple LLM judges and aggregate their outputs to obtain more reliable assessments. Although effective, these multi-judge approaches incur substantial inference-time overhead, which hinders scalability and real-time use in large-scale dialogue evaluation.

To address these challenges, we propose an efficient multi-turn dialogue evaluator that learns from multiple LLM judges. Given that pairwise comparisons are more reliable than direct scoring for LLMs (Cui et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Feng

- et al., 2025), we first construct a large-scale pairwise preference dataset for multi-turn dialogues, with each dialogue annotated by multiple advanced LLM judges. We then develop a lightweight evaluator composed of a text-embedding model with specialized scoring heads, using a learning-to-rank training strategy (Liu et al., 2009). In particular, we explicitly incorporate a maximum likelihood estimation approach to jointly optimize the evaluator and model the reliability of each judge. Extensive experiments on various multi-turn dialogue benchmarks show that our model exhibits strong robustness and wide applicability, surpassing existing baselines across various evaluation tasks. Our main contributions are as follows:

- • We propose a learning framework that effectively aggregates preference data from multiple LLM judges into a single evaluator. This reference-free method retains the strengths of diverse multi-judge feedback while substantially reducing computation costs.
- • We develop MTDEval, a lightweight opensource model that can efficiently and flexibly evaluate multi-turn dialogues for both single rating and pairwise comparison tasks. MTDEval supports evaluations at overall or fine-grained levels across ten commonly used dialogue quality dimensions.

- • We construct P2-MTD, a large-scale preference dataset for multi-turn dialogues, where each response pair is annotated by multiple advanced LLM judges across ten fine-grained dimensions as well as an overall quality, providing rich supervision for training evaluators.
- • We release Daily-MTD, a high-quality human-annotated benchmark comprising 600 multi-turn dialogues focused on daily-life scenarios. Each dialogue is annotated with overall ratings, pairwise preferences, and finegrained judgments across ten evaluation dimensions.

### 2 Related Work

- 2.1 LLM-based Dialogue Evaluation

In the past two years, LLM-as-a-judge (Zheng et al., 2023; Gu et al., 2024; Li et al., 2023c; Chan et al., 2023; Thakur et al., 2024) has gained attention as a promising paradigm to mimic the depth and granularity of human in multi-turn dialogue evaluation (Kim et al., 2024). Inspired by the high correlations with human evaluation capabilities of advanced proprietary LLMs, recent research (Wang et al.,

- 2023; Zeng et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2023; Lee et al.,

2024) has emphasized the development of opensource LLM evaluators. Prometheus2 (Kim et al., 2024) enhances adaptability to custom evaluation criteria via weight merging, supporting both direct assessment and pairwise ranking tasks. SaMer (Feng et al., 2025) introduces a scenario-aware, multi-dimensional evaluation framework that dynamically selects relevant evaluation dimensions based on the specific dialogue scenario, thereby enabling both fine-grained and overall assessment. However, these models primarily focus on singleturn dialogue evaluation. Although they can be extended to assess multi-turn dialogues, their performance in this setting is notably degraded. We aim to fill this gap by introducing MTDEval, a dialogue evaluator specifically trained for multi-turn dialogue quality assessment.

2.2 Multi-LLM Aggregation for Evaluation A range of aggregation methods (Rahmani et al.,

- 2024; Sun et al., 2024a; Wei et al., 2024b; Sun et al., 2024b) have been widely explored for LLMbased evaluation. Simple methods such as majority voting (Bai et al., 2024), averaging probabilities (Sun et al., 2024a), and Crowdlayer (Rodrigues

and Pereira, 2018) have demonstrated the effectiveness of leveraging multiple LLMs to obtain robust results. Recent studies, such as PoLL (Verga et al., 2024), utilize a straightforward aggregation approach by assigning equal weight to each evaluator. Similarly, PRD (Li et al., 2023b) employs multiple LLMs for pairwise ranking. Despite their effectiveness, these methods entail substantial computational overhead due to the need for repeated inference across multiple models, which limits their applicability in large-scale or real-time scenarios. In this study, we aim to develop an efficient evaluator by learning from multiple LLM judges.

### 3 Dataset Construction

#### 3.1 Training Dataset: P2-MTD

Since existing pairwise preference datasets focus on single-turn dialogues and typically annotated by a single judge (Havrilla, 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Ji et al., 2024), in this study, we construct P2-MTD, a novel Pairwise Preference-annotated Multi-Turn Dialogue dataset, where each dialogue pair is evaluated across ten fine-grained evaluation dimensions plus an overall rating by multiple LLM judges.

Specifically, we construct P2-MTD based on the Multiturn-Chat-0.8M dataset (Wen et al., 2023), which contains 831K Chinese multi-turn dialogues. We employ five advanced LLMs as judges, namely Claude-3.7-Sonnet (Anthropic, 2024), GPT-4o (OpenAI et al., 2024), Grok-3 (Grok, 2025), DeepSeek-R1 (DeepSeek-AI, 2025), and Gemini2.0-Flash (Team et al., 2023). Each judge is guided by a standardized evaluation template, which requires them to first provide a detailed explanation, followed by an evaluation along ten fine-grained dimensions, i.e., Accuracy, Logicality, Conversationality, Relevance, Personalization, Creativity, Interactivity, Emotionality, Informativeness, and Safety. Each dimension, along with the overall score, is rated on a three-alternative forced choice: “A”, “B”, or “Fair”. The data pre-processing procedures are provided in Appendix A.

Finally, we construct a multi-judge annotated preference dataset comprising ∼11K multi-turn dialogues. Each instance consists of two dialogues responding to the same user query, along with preference annotations {rj}Mj=1 from M = 5 LLM judges. Each judge j provides evaluations rj = {r1j,r2j,...,rKj ,rallj } where K = 10 repre-

sents fine-grained evaluation dimensions and rallj denotes the overall preference. This dataset is then

used for training a dialogue quality evaluator.

#### 3.2 Evaluation Benchmark: Daily-MTD

To provide a reliable benchmark for evaluating multi-turn dialogue quality, we introduce a novel high-quality human-annotated evaluation dataset named Daily-MTD, consisting of 600 carefully curated multi-turn dialogues in daily-life scenarios. Specifically, we recruited ten undergraduate students majoring in Computer Science to participate in dialogue collection tasks. Each participant interacted with two distinct chatbots, engaging in conversations ranging from 2 to 10 turns. This process yielded an initial pool of 1,080 multi-turn dialogues. Subsequently, five NLP researchers independently selected 600 diverse dialogues from the initial collection. Each expert annotates the dialogues across 10 fine-grained dimensions, along with an overall quality rating. The final labels for single rating were obtained by score averaging, while those for pairwise and multi-dimensional comparisons were determined via majority voting. Finally, we constructed a high-quality human-annotated evaluation dataset, adapted into three specialized forms to accommodate different assessment tasks: DailyMTD for single rating, Daily-MTD-Pair for pairwise comparison, and Daily-MTD-Dim for multidimensional comparison. More details about DailyMTD are presented in Appendix B.

By leveraging pairwise signals from multiple LLM judges, our goal is to learn an efficient evaluator on D that estimates dialogue quality. MTDEval consists of a frozen embedding model fg parameterized by g and a trainable quality predictor fω with parameters ω that comprises two multi-layer perceptron (MLP) heads: (i) a K-dimensional head producing fine-grained scores {fω◦g,k(x)}Kk=1, and (ii) an overall head producing fω◦g,all(x). Both heads are trained under the same formulation below, conditioning on the corresponding k. The construction of D is described in Section 3.1.

#### 4.2 Probabilistic Modeling

Let qk(x) denote the (latent) quality of a multi-turn dialogue x on dimension k, and zk ∈ {0,1} denote the latent ground-truth preference label on dimension k for a dialogue pair (A,B). To model uncertainty in dialogue quality, we adopt Thurstone’s Case V model (Thurstone, 2017). Specifically, we assume that qk(x) follows a Gaussian distribution with mean fω◦g,k(x) and standard deviation σ, i.e., qk(x) ∼ N(fω◦g,k(x),σ). ### 5 Experiments

In this section, we demonstrate the effectiveness of MTDEval across comprehensive dialogue evaluation tasks. We first describe our implementation setup (Section 5.1) and evaluation datasets and metrics covering single rating, pairwise comparison, and multi-dimensional comparison tasks (Section 5.2). We then present the main results

Finally, we conduct ablation studies on the effects of judge composition (Section 5.5), analyze the learned reliability parameters of different judges (Section 5.6), and compare inference efficiency against baseline approaches (Section 5.7).

#### 5.1 Implementation of MTDEval

We employ the Llama-3-8B as the text embedding model g and initialize it with parameters from a trained Llama-3-8B reward model (Wang et al., 2024). An MLP layer ω, serving as a quality predictor, is appended to the embedding model. This MLP is optimized using the loss defined in Eq. (9), while keeping the embedding model frozen. During training, we initially set σ = 1 in Eq. (1), and α = β = 0.5 in Eq. (2) and (3). The model is trained for 3 epochs with a batch size of 32. More training details are presented in Appendix C.

#### 5.2 Evaluation Datasets and Metrics

We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of MTDEval using three types of benchmarks: overalllevel single rating and pairwise comparison, as well as multi-dimensional comparison. Detailed descriptions of the evaluation datasets are provided in Appendix D.

- • Single Rating: This paradigm evaluates the response independently, enabling absolute quality assessment without reliance on comparative baselines. We measure the correlation between model-generated scores and human reference ratings using Pearson and Spearman coefficients. Evaluations are performed on three benchmarks: xDial-IEval (Svikhnushina et al., 2022), MT-Bench (Zheng et al., 2023), and Daily-MTD.
- • Pairwise Comparison: This protocol assesses an evaluator’s ability to predict human preferences between two competing responses. We adopt two evaluation modes: one excluding tie cases (w/o tie) and another including them (w/ tie), to evaluate both binary and ternary judgment accuracy. The evaluation is conducted on four benchmarks: xDialIEval-Pair (Svikhnushina et al., 2022), MTBench-Human (Zheng et al., 2023), ChatbotArena (Chiang et al., 2024), and Daily-MTDPair.

### 6 Conclusion

In this work, we present a learning framework for multi-turn dialogue evaluation that aggregates preference knowledge from multiple LLM judges into a single evaluator. Built upon this framework, we develop MTDEval, an efficient evaluator capable of flexible assessment of dialogue quality through both single rating and pairwise compar-

ison. Moreover, we contribute two high-quality resources to the community: P2-MTD, a largescale multi-judge annotated preference dataset for training dialogue evaluators, and Daily-MTD, a human-annotated evaluation benchmark with finegrained annotations across ten dialogue quality dimensions. Extensive experiments have demonstrated that MTDEval outperforms existing baselines in terms of accuracy, robustness, and inference efficiency.

### Limitations

Despite these advances, several limitations remain. First, our reliance on multiple SOTA LLMs as judges for fine-grained preferences inevitably incurs substantial annotation costs, which may reduce the feasibility of scaling the training dataset to larger scales. Additionally, current training data is primarily focused on daily-life scenarios, potentially limiting generalization to more specialized domains. For future work, we plan to expand the coverage of evaluation scenarios and multilingual dialogues, and develop a more nuanced evaluation framework with cost-efficient supervision to further enhance the generalization of learned evaluators.

### Appendix A Details of P2-MTD Construction

#### A.1 Dataset Preprocessing

The detailed preprocessing procedures for P2-MTD are as follows:

- • We filtered the data to retain multi-turn dialogues consisting of 2 to 10 turns.
- • The dialogue direction is naturally controlled by the user without interference from the assistant, and the content is free from excessive technical terminology or irrelevant information through keyword-based selection; for example, dialogue turns containing only trivial utterances such as “Hello,” “Sorry,” or “Thank you” were excluded.
- • Leveraging DeepSeek-V3 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2024), we automatically filtered dialogues that maintain thematic consistency, logical coherence, and smooth transitions between responses, and then translated them into English.
- • Different models (gpt-4o-mini (OpenAI et al., 2024), Qwen2-72B-Instruct (Yang et al., 2024), gemini-1.5-flash (Team et al., 2024), and claude-3.5-sonnet (Anthropic, 2024)) were used to generate alternative responses with noticeable quality differences. To mitigate length bias, we further ensured that the difference in response lengths between each response pair did not exceed 10 words.

#### A.2 Data Annotation

The following details outline the specific steps taken during data annotation:

- • To prevent potential overfitting and selfpreference, where an LLM evaluator rates its own output higher than others’ while human judges would consider them of equal quality (Panickssery et al., 2024), we specifically select five SOTA LLMs distinct from those used during the data generation process as judges.
- • To eliminate position bias, we swapped the A/B order of each dialogue pair, retaining only those samples where all five judges provided consistent outputs after the swap.

• To ensure a balanced distribution of annotation labels, the final dataset was further filtered to contain 40% “A”, 40% “B” and 20% “Fair” annotations.

The following prompt was used to annotate the training data for multi-turn dialogues, both finegrained and overall.

Accuracy: Ensure factual consistency and reliability throughout the dialogue. Avoid hallucinations, contradictions, or misleading content. Responses should interpret questions accurately and provide appropriate support.

Personalization: Responses should exhibit a unique and expressive style. If user preferences

Emotionality: Recognize and adapt to emotional cues in conversation. Express emotions appropriately (e.g., humor, empathy) to maintain warm and natural dialogue.

Informativeness: Dynamically assess the user’s need for depth. Provide verifiable, valuable knowledge rather than shallow or generic answers.

Safety: Continuously monitor risks across the dialogue. Avoid suggestive traps, follow ethical norms, and prevent misleading, offensive, or unsafe responses.

### B Details of Daily-MTD Construction

Ten undergraduate students majoring in Computer Science were recruited to participate in dialogue data collection tasks. Each participant engaged in interactions with two distinct chatbots, generating dialogues consisting of 2 to 10 conversational turns. Participants received a $10 gift card upon completing every 15 dialogue tasks, with each student eligible to contribute up to 120 dialogues. Dialogue tasks were administered through personalized links, each directing the participants to a designated set of tasks hosted on an internal chat interface deployed on a local server. This procedure resulted in an initial corpus of 1,080 multi-turn dialogues.

Subsequently, five experts in the field of NLP independently curated a representative set of 600 diverse dialogues from the initial collection. Each expert performed annotations across 10 fine-grained dimensions, accompanied by an overall quality rating, using the same annotation prompt detailed in Appendix A.2. Annotators received compensation at a rate of $5 per 10 dialogues annotated, totaling $750. The annotation results demonstrated substantial inter-annotator agreement; specifically, in terms of overall quality ratings, 45% of the dialogues received unanimous ratings from all five annotators, 25% had agreement from four annotators, and 30% from three annotators. Final labels for single-rating evaluations were determined via score averaging, whereas labels for pairwise and multi-dimensional comparisons were established through majority voting. Ultimately, we developed a high-quality, human-annotated evaluation dataset, structured into three specialized subsets catering

to distinct evaluation tasks: Daily-MTD for singlerating tasks, Daily-MTD-Pair for pairwise comparisons, and Daily-MTD-Dim for multi-dimensional assessments.

### C Details of MTDEval Training

The optimization of MTDEval is facilitated by the DeepSpeed library (Rasley et al., 2020), leveraging the ZeRO Stage 2 optimizer (Rajbhandari et al., 2020) and FlashAttention2 (Dao, 2023) to achieve efficient parallelism across two NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPUs. We utilize the AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2017), configured with β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.95, and a weight decay of 0.1. The primary learning rate for the model is set to 5 × 10−5, whereas the learning rates for α and β are specified as 1 × 10−2. A linear warm-up is applied during the initial 10% of training steps, followed by a cosine decay schedule to zero. We employ a batch size of 32 and limit the maximum sequence length to 8,192 tokens. The model is trained for 3 epochs to ensure convergence and optimal performance.

The proposed method supports both overall and fine-grained scoring. Accordingly, we trained two distinct models in our experiments: one for overall rating and another for evaluating the performance across specific dimensions.

### D Introduction of Evaluation Datasets

We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of MTDEval using three types of benchmarks: overalllevel single rating and pairwise comparison, as well as multi-dimensional comparison.

Single Rating is regarded as an effective evaluation strategy as it dispenses with the need for comparative baselines. However, its reliance on the LLM’s intrinsic judgment may introduce subjectivity and overlook nuanced distinctions. In single rating, we use Pearson and Spearman as performance metrics to measure scoring correlations with reference answers using a reference-based method. The three Single Rating benchmarks are:

• xDial-IEval (Svikhnushina et al., 2022): A multi-turn dialogue evaluation benchmark consisting of 1,920 dialogue instances and 8 score rubrics, with each dialogue averaging 6 turns, rated by human evaluators on a scale from 1 to 5.

- • MT-Bench (Zheng et al., 2023): A multi-turn chat benchmark that consists of 80 curated prompts, 80 expert-defined scoring rubrics (Kim et al., 2023); and 320 model responses generated by WizardLM-13B, Vicuna-13B, LLaMA-2-13B-Chat, and GPT-3.5-Turbo0613.
- • Daily-MTD: A meticulously constructed multi-turn daily dialogue benchmark consisting of 600 instances, 10 fine-grained score rubrics, each independently annotated by five experts specializing in NLP. The evaluation is conducted on a scale from 1 to 10, and the final score for each instance is derived through a score averaging method across all judges.

Pairwise Comparison reduces subjectivity while enabling the identification of finer distinctions by comparing model-generated response pairs. In pairwise comparison, we use two evaluation approaches: first, excluding "tie" cases (denoted as ‘w/o tie’); second, grouping "tie" responses for evaluation (denoted as ‘w/ tie’). And we use a reference-free method to measure the alignment between model judgments and human judgments. The four Pairwise Comparison benchmarks are:

- • xDial-IEval-Pair (Svikhnushina et al., 2022): A multi-turn benchmark that leverages the original xDial-IEval dataset. For each set of four identical user queries, two response pairs are randomly selected and labeled as ‘win’, ‘fair’, or ‘lose’ based on their respective scores, resulting in the creation of 480 pairwise comparison instances.
- • MT-Bench-Human (Zheng et al., 2023): A multi-turn benchmark that utilizes the same 80 test prompts as MT-Bench. Furthermore, it provides 3,360 response pairs, which are judged by human evaluators as ‘win’, ‘tie’, or ‘lose’.
- • Chatbot-Arena (Chiang et al., 2024): A benchmark that consists of 30K arena data, from which we randomly sample 2K multiturn dialogues. The dataset includes responses from models such as GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Claude, Vicuna-7B/13B, Koala-13B, LLaMA13B. All judgments are provided by collected crowd judges.
- • Daily-MTD-Pair: A benchmark that utilizes the original Daily-MTD dataset. For each user

query, two response pairs A/B are available, and based on their respective scores, they are labeled as "A", "Fair", or "B", which leads to the generation of 300 pairs.

Multi-Dimensional Comparison requires evaluators to generate independent preference judgments for response pairs based on specific dimensions. To assess this capability, we constructed the Daily-MTD-Dim dataset using the annotation information for two alternative responses to each user query in the original Daily-MTD dataset across ten dimensions, ultimately yielding 300 pairs.

### F Ethical Statement

This study aims to advance the reliability and efficiency of automatic evaluation methods for multiturn dialogue systems. The data used in MTDEval primarily target everyday-life scenarios, and the supervision signals are mainly derived from preference judgments generated by multiple SOTA LLMs, together with controlled annotations provided by human experts. This design ensures that no privacy or data confidentiality risks are involved.

We emphasize that MTDEval is strictly a researchoriented evaluation model intended for assessing multi-turn dialogues and is not designed for deployment in real-world, high-risk settings. Given its reliance on synthetic supervision and simulated evaluation environments, this study is not expected to raise significant ethical concerns.
